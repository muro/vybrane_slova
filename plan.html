Vybrané slová PWA plan

A practice app for children around ages 7-10 choosing i/í or y/ý, designed as a sorting game with installable offline PWA behavior.

Target Audience

The app is for children around ages 7-10, with parents or teachers likely nearby. The experience should feel like a clear school-friendly game: playful, calm, readable, and forgiving.

Reading level

Use short Slovak labels, concrete words, and minimal instructions. Show the task through motion and layout instead of long explanatory copy.

Motor skills

Make the card itself the main control. Dragging should be forgiving, with a clear threshold and visible edge hints before an answer is submitted.

Feedback tone

Celebrate correct answers briefly and correct mistakes gently. Avoid shame, pressure, streak loss drama, or anything that makes practice feel punitive.

Core Interaction

The app should feel almost entirely about one word card moving. The word card stays in the center, and the child drags it left for i/í or right for y/ý.

Static destination tiles and framed board panels should not be visible in the main version. Use one page background, with the current word card as the only framed surface. On drag start, the screen edges give a subtle color hint; as the card moves closer to one side, that side becomes stronger and the opposite side becomes quieter. The missing letter is filled directly inside the word while dragging so the child sees what will be submitted.

Keep the language as moving, dragging, and choosing an answer. Avoid copy such as "swipe left", "swipe right", "like", "reject", "match", or "nope".

Expected Controls

Primary input

Drag the card horizontally. Submit only after a meaningful sideways distance so accidental tiny movements snap back.

Minimal chrome

The header should contain only a letter-set chooser, defaulting to all letters, and an about button for feedback and project links.

Feedback

Correct answers turn the card into a small confetti burst. Incorrect answers split the card into falling strips and use a short corrective message without shame.

Game Loop

  1. Choose a lesson or continue the current practice set.
  2. Show the next word card in the center with the target letter blanked out.
  3. The child drags the card left or right. The blank fills with the hinted letter during the drag.
  4. If the card crosses the drag threshold, submit the chosen answer; otherwise snap back.
  5. For correct answers, let the card become a small confetti burst. For mistakes, split the card into falling strips, reveal the correct full word, and keep the correction gentle.
  6. Advance until the round is complete, then show score, streak, and next-step recommendation.

Keep routine progress indicators out of the main screen unless they are needed for a round-complete state. The main screen should stay centered on the card.

Content Model

Use contrast pairs first. Skip unpaired selected words in the first version, because the app should teach the choice between two meaningful spellings, not only ask for memorized isolated words.

Picture-friendly starter pairs

výr / vír Owl on a tree versus water whirl. Strong first-pack picture contrast.
syr / síra Use kus syra for syr. For síra, use a match, a yellow sulfur rock, or a simple atom/molecule drawing so the concept is visible.
ryža / ríša Bowl of rice versus castle, crown, or fairy-tale kingdom.
vyje / vije Dog or wolf howling versus weaving a wreath or braid.
vyla / víla Howling animal or character versus fairy. Needs sentence context but can still be visual.
pichá / pýcha Needle, thorn, or cactus pricking versus a proud pose. Keep the pýcha sentence gentle.

Context-first pairs

Pairs such as my / mi, milý / mýli, byť / biť, and odbyť / odbiť need sentence context more than pictures. They can appear after the visual pairs.

Current count

The documented starter set currently has 10 contrast pairs, or 20 surface-form cards: 6 picture-friendly pairs (12 cards) and 4 context-first pairs (8 cards). Because each pair has one y/ý form and one i/í form, the current set is balanced at 10 y/ý cards and 10 i/í cards.

The ordinary non-disambiguation list is now split into compact per-letter plain word lists with 388 candidate words by convention: 188 y/ý selected-word rows and 200 i/í control rows. Words that need context or picture disambiguation live separately in the rich disambiguation list, which currently has 22 rows: 20 ready starter cards and 2 review cards for by / bi.

Future packs must include enough i/í cards that children cannot learn "this app usually wants y". Prefer balanced packs, or packs with more i/í controls than selected-word y/ý cards when adding unpaired drill content.

Form scope

For the first full selected-word list, include only basic dictionary forms. Do not generate declensions, cases, plural forms, or conjugated verb forms as separate cards yet. The starter contrast cards may still use a specific surface form when the contrast itself is normally taught or understood that way, but broad coverage should start from base forms.

Add declined and conjugated forms later as an explicit expansion pack after the base list is reviewed and the app flow is working.

Excluded for v1

Skip uncommon or fringe contrasts such as pýtať sa / pita. The first version should use words a 7-10-year-old is likely to know or can understand from a simple sentence or picture.

Reviewable source format

Create source lists that are easy to review in a spreadsheet or diff. Keep non-disambiguation words in plain word-list files under data/words/simple/, using filenames such as b.txt, m.txt, and p.txt. Each simple file uses [i] and [y] sections, with one word or short phrase per word line; the selected letter is inferred from the filename.

Keep the richer pair format only in data/words/disambiguation.tsv, with one row per surface form for words where both spellings/forms exist. Columns: id, pack, pair_id, selected_letter, surface, answer_group, contrast_surface, needs_disambiguation, disambiguation_mode, picture_role, sentence, image_prompt, source, status, and notes.

Use disambiguation_mode values such as picture, sentence, or both. Use picture_role to say whether the image works for both_sides, this_side_only, contrast_side_only, or none. It is fine when only one word from a pair has a useful picture; the other side can rely on a sentence fragment.

Add a simple validator test before using the lists in the app. It should fail on malformed simple word-list files, unknown answer_group, unknown disambiguation_mode, unknown picture_role, duplicate card IDs, broken pair references, simple-list words that also appear in the disambiguation file, and packs whose i/í versus y/ý distribution violates the balance rule.

Work Tracks

Task 1: Define Word Lists

Owns the reviewable simple word lists, TSV files, sources, starter packs, simple y/ý rows, i/í control words, disambiguation tags, picture prompts, sentence fragments, and validator rules.

Done means the selected pack has clear review status, balanced answers, age-appropriate sentences, and enough metadata for the app to load without guessing.

Task 2: Build The App

Owns the PWA shell, data loader, engine, drag-card interaction, animations, scoring, persistence, tests, preview page, manifest, service worker, and icons.

The app should consume only rows whose status and pack are intentionally enabled. It must ignore unreviewed candidate rows by default.

Boundary

Do not hide content decisions inside app code. If a word needs a better sentence, picture, source, or review status, fix the TSV row and validator; the app should stay data-driven.

What To Reuse From Clockwork

Clockwork is a good model because it keeps the app small, static, installable, and testable. Reuse these ideas as product architecture, while replacing all clock-specific logic and visuals.

One folder to ship Put the runtime app in web/ and make that folder the deployable website artifact. No npm install, no build step, no CDN dependency required for the first version.
Installable PWA shell Start from Clockwork's index.html PWA shape: mobile viewport, viewport-fit=cover, theme color, Apple home-screen meta tags, manifest link, icon links, local scripts, and service-worker registration.
Relative manifest paths Use Clockwork's manifest pattern so it works wherever it is hosted on the website: start_url: "./index.html", scope: "./", display: "standalone", portrait orientation, theme/background colors, and 192/512 PNG icons.
Offline app shell Reuse the small sw.js strategy: precache the app shell, cache same-origin GET requests, fall back to index.html for navigation, and bump a named cache version when shipped assets change.
Local runtime files If the UI uses React like Clockwork, commit the web/vendor/ runtime files and load them locally. That keeps the installed app reliable offline and avoids third-party runtime fetches.
Pure engine.js Keep word data, answer-checking, lesson generation, scoring, mastery, and localStorage helpers in engine.js. The UI should ask the engine questions, not contain spelling rules.
Data-driven lessons Model lessons as data with stable keys, similar to Clockwork's level table. Adding or reordering a lesson should be a data edit, and stored progress should survive display-order changes.
Browser tests Reuse the tests.html pattern. Tests should load engine.js directly and cover word data, accepted answers, lesson selection, scoring thresholds, localStorage migration, and progress reset.
Headless-friendly checks Copy the idea of a simple test summary in the document title or DOM. It makes local automation easy because a script can open tests.html and read pass/fail without a full test framework.
Namespaced storage Use versioned localStorage keys such as vybrane-slova.progress.v1, vybrane-slova.settings.v1, and vybrane-slova.lesson. Plan migrations up front so word-set changes do not strand old progress.
Offline about sheet Reuse Clockwork's in-app about/support sheet pattern: keep the sheet inside the PWA, include a feedback mail link, link to aidistillery.dev, and avoid requiring network just to explain the app.
Dev-only phone preview Reuse preview.html as a phone-frame wrapper for visual checks, but keep the shipped app unframed and full-screen.
Generated app icons Reuse the icon-generation workflow from tools/build_icon.py, but generate a new identity around word cards, answer piles, or Slovak spelling practice.
Installed-mobile polish Reuse 100dvh, safe-area padding with env(safe-area-inset-*), tap-highlight removal, and overscroll control so the installed app feels native on phones.

What Not To Reuse Directly

  • Do not reuse Clockwork's clock engine, time curriculum, dandelion assets, or clock icon.
  • Do not carry over a visual theme just because it exists; this app should feel like a focused Slovak spelling practice game.
  • Do not depend on hidden gestures as the only input. The side piles should always be tappable.

Implementation Phases

  1. Static PWA skeleton. Add web/index.html, web/manifest.webmanifest, web/sw.js, icons, and optional web/preview.html.
  2. Word engine. Build web/engine.js with lesson data, answer checking, randomization, round scoring, and localStorage helpers.
  3. Three-pile UI. Build the center pile and the two answer piles, including tap, drag/flick, keyboard, and the card movement animation.
  4. Feedback and progression. Add correct/incorrect feedback, round summaries, streaks, mastery tracking, and a gentle reset flow.
  5. Offline and install polish. Finish service-worker caching, icon generation, safe-area behavior, tests, and deployment notes.
  6. Later morphology pack. After the base-form list is reviewed, add declined noun/adjective forms and conjugated verb forms as a separate content expansion with its own review pass and validator coverage.

Open Decisions

The main product decision still needed is the curriculum shape: whether the first version practices only binary i/í vs y/ý, or whether later lessons distinguish short vs long letters too. The UI can support both, but the word data should be modeled deliberately before implementation.
